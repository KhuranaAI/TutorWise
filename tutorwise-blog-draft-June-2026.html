Final Exams and Culminating Tasks: A June Game Plan for Ontario Students

June 15, 2026 TutorWise

June is a busy month for Ontario families. High school students are heading into final exams and culminating projects, and elementary students are wrapping up their last units before the final report card. It is also the month when energy and focus tend to drop just as the stakes go up.

A little planning now can make the difference between a stressful finish and a strong one.

Know What Is Being Evaluated

In Ontario high schools, a student's final mark is usually made up of 70% term work and 30% final evaluation. That final 30% can be an exam, a culminating project, a presentation or a combination of these. It varies by course and by teacher.

Sit down with your teen and list every course along with what the final evaluation looks like and when it is due. Many students are surprised to find that two big tasks land in the same week. Seeing it all in one place is the first step to managing it.

Build a Study Calendar Backwards From Exam Day

Once you know the dates, work backwards. If a math exam is on June 24, decide which units will be reviewed on which days leading up to it. Spread the work out so that no single night is overloaded.

A good rule of thumb is to start reviewing for each exam at least a week in advance, with the heaviest subjects getting the most time. Build in a buffer day or two for anything that takes longer than expected.

Active Studying Beats Rereading

Many students study by rereading their notes. It feels productive, but it does not do much for memory. Active studying works much better.

Practice questions. In math and science, doing problems without looking at the solution is the most effective way to prepare. Past tests and textbook review questions are a great place to start.

Teach it back. Ask your teen to explain a concept to you as if you have never heard of it. Gaps in understanding show up quickly.

Make a one-page summary. Condensing a whole unit onto a single page forces students to decide what really matters.

Culminating Tasks Need Checkpoints Too

Projects and essays are easy to put off because the due date feels far away. Help your teen break the task into smaller steps, such as research, outline, first draft and final edits, and give each step its own deadline.

Encourage them to check the rubric before they start and again before they hand it in. Teachers grade against the rubric, so it is the clearest guide to what earns marks.

Sleep, Food and Phones

It is tempting to stay up late in exam season, but sleep is when the brain locks in what it has learned. A student who sleeps eight hours will often outperform one who studied an extra two hours at midnight.

Regular meals, short breaks and some time outside all help. A twenty-minute walk between study blocks can do more for focus than another coffee or energy drink. Phones are one of the biggest distractions during study time, so suggest putting them in another room during focused work blocks.

What Elementary Families Can Do in June

Younger students do not usually write formal exams, but June still matters. Teachers are finishing their final assessments for the report card, so keep homework and reading routines going until the last week of school.

When the June report card arrives, read the comments as well as the marks. They often point to the exact skills your child should strengthen before next year.
